
Hush v1 Assumptions Register
This register catalogs every unresolved assumption the frozen model rests on, distilled from the spec stack and the accepted Red Team Review. It is ordered by risk — existential first. Each entry is stated so that an engineer or scientist could, in principle, design a test for it. Where the honest answer is "we cannot test this in the MVP," I say so plainly, because a register that hides its blind spots is worse than none.
A note on what "existential" means here: an assumption is existential if its falsity invalidates the product's core claim — that Hush can earn the right to decide — not merely a feature. Four assumptions meet that bar. They are the ones to read carefully.

EXISTENTIAL
A1 — Capability is recoverable from confounded observations
Assumption: True capability can be inferred from observed = capability − fatigue despite two unknowns and one equation per observation, because fatigue is computable from training history. Why it exists: The entire learning loop (ES-005.1, ES-011) requires a capability estimate; without separating capability from fatigue, every observation mis-attributes recovery state to capability change. What would falsify it: Capability scores that fail to predict out-of-sample performance better than a naive "last working set" heuristic; or score trajectories that diverge depending on the order/spacing of sessions when true capability was constant. What data would validate it: Periodic standardized fresh-state tests (same exercise, long rest, controlled conditions) showing the inferred capability matches the fresh-state measurement. The system does not currently schedule these. Testable in MVP? Partially, and only if fresh-state probes are added. Without deliberate standardized re-tests, this is untestable — the model can explain any outcome as fatigue/effort/confidence, so nothing falsifies it. This is the Red Team's deepest finding: a model that explains everything predicts nothing. Risk: Existential.
A2 — The coupled learning loop converges
Assumption: The composition of all feedback loops (capability ↔ prediction ↔ load ↔ observation ↔ fatigue ↔ confidence) is globally stable, despite each loop being tuned independently. Why it exists: Each spec proved local damping (small learning rate, conservative discount, slow blend); global stability was assumed to follow. What would falsify it: Capability scores or recommended loads that oscillate, ratchet, or drift over long horizons for an athlete whose true capability is stable; sensitivity of long-run trajectory to small parameter changes. What data would validate it: 6–18 months of per-athlete trajectories showing convergence to stable values, plus sensitivity analysis across parameter perturbations. Testable in MVP? No. The validation window (8–12 weeks) is shorter than the 23-week evidence-decay half-life (ES-005.1 §6). The mechanism that governs long-run behavior is never even half-exercised. Ships unvalidated. Risk: Existential.
A3 — Trust is measurable through behavior
Assumption: Earned trust is distinguishable from passive compliance via behavioral signals (costly-case compliance, override variance, discernment), per ES-012. Why it exists: The product's entire purpose is earning the right to decide; ES-012 must operationalize "trust" or the company has no success metric. What would falsify it: Athletes whose behavioral trust signals (high costly-deference, retained discernment) are uncorrelated with long-term retention or self-reported trust; inability to distinguish "thoughtful agreement" from "stopped thinking" in any data. What data would validate it: Behavioral trust scores that predict retention and survive correlation with independent trust measures, across enough costly-case events per athlete. Testable in MVP? No, for a meaningful fraction of the cohort. Trust requires DECREASE_LOAD and fatigue-hold events that the conservative system is designed to avoid generating (Red Team §1.4). Many athletes will reach week 8 having never experienced a costly recommendation, leaving their TrustScore undefined. DiscernmentRetained is unobservable in principle (§3.4). The North Star is partially unmeasurable. Risk: Existential.
A4 — Athletes will delegate decisions to a system
Assumption: The primary hypothesis (Founder Ch9) — athletes are willing to hand training decisions to software that earns trust — is true for a population broader than self-selecting early adopters. Why it exists: It is the founding premise; everything else is execution conditional on it. What would falsify it: Even among engaged athletes, persistent override of the system's authority on consequential decisions, or churn driven by "I want control" rather than "it was wrong." What data would validate it: Sustained costly-deference across a non-self-selected sample; qualitative evidence that delegation (not novelty or compliance) drives retention. Testable in MVP? Weakly. Beta users self-select into delegation (Red Team §selection bias), so the MVP measures the disposition of people already inclined to defer — it cannot generalize. Survivorship (§6.5) further biases upward. Risk: Existential. This is the one assumption that, if false, no amount of model quality saves.

HIGH
A5 — effort_offset and fatigue are separately identifiable
Assumption: The stable effort bias and time-varying fatigue can be disentangled by their timescales (ES-011 C.4). Why it exists: Both produce systematic negative prediction error; without separation they double-correct and the score drifts. What would falsify it: An athlete whose effort genuinely drifts over weeks (e.g., grows comfortable training closer to failure) — the slow effort drift becomes indistinguishable from a long-τ fatigue component, and the two corrections trade mass arbitrarily. What data would validate it: Independent measure of effort (RIR/proximity-to-failure) to anchor the decomposition — which the product refuses to collect (Anti-Requirement). Testable in MVP? No. Requires the exact input the product forbids. Unidentifiable from rep count alone. Risk: High.
A6 — Per-athlete recovery rates (τ) are learnable
Assumption: 8 recovery half-lives per athlete (7 capability + 1 systemic) can be estimated from training data (ES-011 D.3). Why it exists: Recovery is athlete-specific; fixed population τ mis-times progression for fast/slow recoverers. What would falsify it: τ estimates that fail to stabilize, or that fit noise (high variance across re-fits on the same athlete). What data would validate it: Many observations per capability at varied rest intervals — requires deliberate rest-interval variation. Testable in MVP? No. ~5 observations per capability at one or two habitual rest intervals cannot identify 8 decay constants (Red Team §4.2). Recommendation: ship fixed population τ; do not enable per-athlete learning until validated. Risk: High.
A7 — Conservative seeds are safe across all cohorts
Assumption: ES-008 v2 seeds (cohort-adjusted, discounted) produce safe, non-failure first sessions for every real athlete. Why it exists: First-session failure is the fastest trust-destroyer (ES-003 Principle #39); seeds must lean light. What would falsify it: First-session fail-on-first-rep events, especially for under-represented cohorts (older, female, detrained, bodyweight-dominant movements). What data would validate it: First-session completion and failure rates by cohort. Testable in MVP? Yes — and this is the single most testable high-risk assumption. Week-1 data directly measures it. Prioritize cohort diversity in recruitment so the tails are represented. Risk: High (but cheaply testable — invest here first).
A8 — The de-biased error is a valid model-quality signal
Assumption: Fatigue- and effort-adjusted prediction error (ES-012 D.1) honestly measures learning, not artifact. Why it exists: Raw error conflates model quality with effort and recovery; the de-biased series is the only "honest" signal. What would falsify it: Error decreasing only because capability variance collapsed (regression to the mean), not because the model learned; or because the corrections (fatigue, effort) absorb the error rather than the model improving. What data would validate it: De-biased error beating the shadow baseline while capability is still moving (ES-012 D.3). Testable in MVP? Weakly. The shadow-baseline comparison cannot be powered at N=10–100 with per-athlete models (Red Team §6.4). You can compute it directionally; you cannot reach significance. Risk: High — because this is the metric that distinguishes "learned" from "users defer," and it's underpowered.
A9 — Overrides are interpretable
Assumption: When an athlete overrides, the system knows what they did (exercise, load) well enough to attribute it (ES-010). Why it exists: Override-as-evidence is central to learning and to ES-012's override-productivity. What would falsify it: A meaningful fraction of overrides to off-catalog exercises (no difficulty_factor, no capability mapping) → uninterpretable observations. What data would validate it: Measured rate of off-catalog substitutions and their handling. Testable in MVP? Yes. Log override targets; measure off-catalog fraction directly. The 40–60 exercise catalog (ES-002) vs. real-gym variety makes this likely material. Risk: High.

MEDIUM
A10 — κ (set→fatigue scaling) can be set meaningfully
Assumption: A single global κ converts set work to fatigue in score units sensibly. Why it exists: Fatigue must be quantified to be removed. What would falsify it: No single κ producing stable, predictive fatigue across exercises and athletes; κ absorbing unrelated model error. What data would validate it: Within-session rep-decline patterns matching κ-predicted fatigue across many sessions. Testable in MVP? Partially. Within-session decline is observable; whether κ generalizes is not, at this N. κ is a "garbage-collector" parameter (Red Team §4.1) — high chance it hides other errors. Risk: Medium.
A11 — σ²_ref (conflict variance reference) is settable
Assumption: A reference variance correctly distinguishes real evidence conflict from normal noise (ES-010 Part C). Why it exists: Confidence suppression on conflict requires a normalizer. What would falsify it: Confidence that either never suppresses (useless) or always suppresses (perma-conservative); high-natural-variance athletes stuck at low confidence permanently. What data would validate it: Population variance distribution to anchor σ²_ref. Testable in MVP? Partially. You can observe whether confidence stabilizes per athlete; you can't validate the population anchor at this N. Interacts with κ and τ (jointly unidentifiable — Red Team §4.3). Risk: Medium.
A12 — Capabilities are sufficiently independent
Assumption: Treating the 7 capabilities as independent (no learned cross-correlation in V1, ES-Founder Ch3) loses little. Why it exists: Independence is a deliberate V1 simplification; correlations are deferred. What would falsify it: Prediction accuracy materially improved by modeling correlations (e.g., OHP progress predicting bench progress). What data would validate it: Residual correlation analysis across capabilities post-hoc. Testable in MVP? Yes, post-hoc. Cheap to analyze residuals even if not acted on. Risk: Medium.
A13 — Volume bands map to real stimulus
Assumption: ES-009.1's low/moderate/high (8/12/18 sets/capability/week) produce meaningfully different, appropriate training stimulus. Why it exists: weekly_volume needed a concrete mapping; nothing consumed the free-text field before. What would falsify it: No difference in capability progression across bands, or bands driving overreaching/under-stimulus. What data would validate it: Capability trend by volume band. Testable in MVP? Weakly. 8 weeks is short for volume-response signal, and bands are confounded with adherence. Risk: Medium.
A14 — Exploration floor prevents preference lock-in
Assumption: 10% exploration (ES-009 §6) is enough to prevent the preference→selection→preference loop from narrowing the exercise pool. Why it exists: Preference is self-reinforcing; exploration is the only counterweight. What would falsify it: Exercise pool narrowing over time despite exploration; dropped exercises never re-tested in practice. What data would validate it: Exercise-diversity-over-time per athlete. Testable in MVP? Partially. 8 weeks may be too short for lock-in to manifest (it's a months-long risk). Red Team judged 10% likely too weak against 90% reinforcement. Risk: Medium.
A15 — Trust exponents (a, b, c) are defensible
Assumption: The multiplicative weighting of TrustScore components (ES-012 C.2) reflects real trust structure. Why it exists: The score needed weights; defaults were chosen. What would falsify it: Different weightings yielding different validation verdicts on the same data. What data would validate it: Outcome data correlating weightings with long-term retention across hundreds of athletes. Testable in MVP? No. Unfalsifiable at this N (Red Team §4.4). Guesses dressed as design. Risk: Medium (low immediate harm, but the North Star inherits the arbitrariness).

LOW
A16 — Epley is an adequate rep model
Assumption: The Epley formula (ES-005.1 §2) adequately maps load↔reps across the relevant range. Why it exists: Needed an invertible, deterministic rep model; Epley is standard. What would falsify it: Systematic prediction bias at rep-range extremes (very low or very high reps). What data would validate it: Residual error vs. rep count. Testable in MVP? Yes, post-hoc. Known to degrade at low reps (Red Team noted quantization there); use relative error bands. Risk: Low.
A17 — The shared-k curve shape holds across capabilities
Assumption: All Class-A capabilities share the same growth constant k (ES-008 v2), making scores percentile-comparable. Why it exists: Shared k is what makes cross-capability comparison valid for ES-006 imbalance detection. What would falsify it: Capabilities whose true strength curves bend differently, breaking percentile equivalence. What data would validate it: Population strength-distribution shape per lift. Testable in MVP? No. Needs population data. But it's a documentation/comparison nicety, not a loop-critical quantity — low blast radius if wrong. Risk: Low.
A18 — Plate/equipment quantization is negligible
Assumption: Rounding loads to equipment increments (ES-005.1 §13) introduces tolerable error. Why it exists: Real gyms have discrete loads. What would falsify it: Quantization-dominated prediction error, especially low-rep/strength work or fixed-stack machines. What data would validate it: Error decomposition isolating quantization. Testable in MVP? Yes, post-hoc. Use relative error bands at low reps. Risk: Low.

The structural truth the register exposes: the four existential assumptions are precisely the four the MVP cannot test — capability recoverability (A1), loop convergence (A2), trust measurability (A3), and willingness to delegate (A4). Everything the MVP can test cleanly (A7 seeds, A9 override interpretability, A12/A16/A18 post-hoc) is Medium or below. The risk and the testability are inversely correlated. You can thoroughly validate the things that won't kill you and barely touch the things that will.
The two moves that change this register most:
First, add deliberate, safe probes — occasional recommendations slightly off the conservative default, and deliberate variation in rest intervals. This is the only intervention that makes A1 (fresh-state capability checks), A3 (manufactures the costly-case events trust needs), and A6 (varies the intervals τ needs) even partially testable. Without probing, three of the four existential assumptions and two High assumptions are untestable by construction. This is the highest-leverage decision available before the trial.
Second, recruit for cohort diversity — A7 (seed safety) is the most dangerous cheaply-testable assumption, and its danger lives entirely in the tails (older, female, detrained, bodyweight movements) that a homogeneous beta cohort would miss. A diverse N=20 tests A7 better than a homogeneous N=100.
The register's honest verdict: Hush v1 can validate that it is safe and coherent in the short run. It cannot, as designed, validate that it is correct in the long run or that it has earned the right to decide — those rest on A1–A4, which the MVP structurally cannot reach. Proceed, but proceed knowing that a successful MVP proves the system is trustworthy-seeming, not that it is trustworthy. The gap between those two is the whole product, and closing it requires either a longer horizon, deliberate probing, or both.

Assumptions Register — re-annotation for the v1 advisory/stagnation pivot.

The assumption SET (A1–A18+), risk levels, and MVP-testability rules are unchanged. The v1 pivot (Hush is advisory; success = stagnation detection + program value, not load authority) shifts how heavily a few assumptions BITE:
A1 (capability recoverable from confounded observations) — LESS CENTRAL to the success claim. Because the system no longer asserts the working load, exact capability recovery is no longer the headline success criterion; what matters is an honest, stable estimate good enough to detect stagnation (M5). A1 still underpins the score, but a miss is less consequential.
A9 (overrides interpretable) — RESTATED: a load deviation is a LEARNING INPUT, not an override to suppress. As of M1 (DX-01/02) the logged actual_weight feeds the same precision-weighted blend (effective_load = actual_weight); quality is evaluated at the actual load. A9 is now 'deviation = input', and it remains fully testable (the logged weight IS the signal). See the ES-010 addendum (DX-13).
A5 (effort/fatigue separable without RIR) — UNCHANGED but HEAVIER. With the load no longer authored by the system, the de-biased error and fatigue attribution lean more on the logged performance; effort_offset still ships at 0 (unidentifiable without RIR, an Anti-Requirement). Every fatigue/effort-dependent conclusion stays flagged un-separated (Invariant 7).
A14 (exploration floor prevents preference lock-in) — INACTIVE. Exploration is OFF by default (P_EXPLORE = 0.0, DX-04); composition is preference-stable. Lock-in mitigation now flows through DX-10 (sticky preference — SPECIFIED, pending) plus M5 imbalance/stagnation detection (DX-09), not a random exploration draw. The mechanism is retained for Phase-0 override study only.
A7 (seed safety across cohorts) — still fully testable week 1; note bodyweight_kg is now collected (DX-07), and the seed target is Option D (DX-08, pending) — until then the 3-bucket seed stands.
